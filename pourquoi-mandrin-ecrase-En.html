Why does a paper core crush during winding?

A core that ovalizes or deforms during winding stops the line and wastes material. Here are the three most common causes, and how to fix them.

1. Wall thickness undersized for the applied tension

This is the most common cause. The core's wall must withstand the radial force the material applies during winding, not just carry the finished roll's weight. A high-tension stretch film can crush a 3 mm core that would hold up fine under light paper.

Fix: increase the wall thickness in steps (for example from 5 to 7 mm) and test it on your actual line before locking in the order.

2. Radial compression strength not properly assessed

A core's radial strength depends on the quality of the kraft board used, the number of plies, and the bonding adhesive. Two cores of the same thickness can have very different strength depending on how they're made.

Fix: ask your supplier for radial compression test results, not just the wall thickness. This lab measurement is what actually predicts how the core will behave under clamping.

3. Poorly controlled moisture

Cardboard is hygroscopic: too much moisture softens the fibers and sharply reduces the core's strength, even if the thickness on paper is correct. Too little moisture, on the other hand, makes the board brittle.

Fix: require a controlled moisture range, typically kept between 4 and 6%, checked per batch with a calibrated hygrometer. Also store your cores away from humidity before use.

How to diagnose it quickly

  • Does the core deform within the first few turns? Suspect the wall thickness first.
  • Does it hold at the start but fail near the end of winding? Check the batch's moisture content.
  • Does the problem only affect some batches, not all? Ask your supplier for that batch's test results.
