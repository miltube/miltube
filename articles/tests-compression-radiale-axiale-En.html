Radial and axial compression: how to read core test results

Two tests let you predict how a core will behave before it reaches your line. Here's what they measure, and why to ask for them before ordering.

The radial compression test

It measures the core's resistance to a force applied from the outside toward the center, similar to the tension a film or paper applies during winding. This is the most relevant test for preventing crushing and ovalization.

It matters most on high-speed converting lines and with high-tension materials such as stretch film.

The axial compression test

It measures the core's load capacity along its length, meaning its resistance to crushing when rolls are stacked vertically on top of each other. This test prevents deformation during storage and transport.

It becomes critical when rolls stay stacked for several weeks, or when they travel long distances.

Two more checks worth knowing

  • Dimensional stability: after thermal conditioning, the core's straightness is measured, with a maximum deviation typically set at 1.5 mm per meter.
  • Moisture content: checked per batch with a calibrated hygrometer, it should stay within a stable range, usually between 4 and 6%.

What to ask your supplier for

Before a volume order, ask for these test results for the specific batch involved, not just a generic data sheet. A supplier that tests every batch in-house can provide them quickly.
