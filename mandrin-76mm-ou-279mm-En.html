76 mm or 279 mm core: how to choose the right range

MilTube produces six standard diameters across three ranges. Here's how to quickly tell which one fits your application.

50 and 60 mm — small format

These diameters suit lightweight materials and small rolls: labels, adhesive tapes, thin stretch film, food packaging foil. They're the most economical diameters to transport and store, suited to fast-cycle packing lines.

76 and 100 mm — industry standard

The 76 mm (3″) diameter is the most widespread standard in the packaging and film industry. It's the right default choice for wide-format printing, general film winding, and common industrial packaging, unless your machine requires a different diameter.

152 and 279 mm — high resistance

These larger diameters support much heavier loads and larger-diameter rolls. They're used for agricultural films, heavy textile, mill-sized paper rolls, and metal coils, where the finished roll's weight far exceeds that of a standard roll.

How to decide quickly

  • If your machine already sets a shaft diameter, that decides it — measure it first.
  • If you're designing a new line, pick the standard diameter closest to your target roll weight and length: the heavier the roll, the wider the diameter should be.
  • If you're torn between two close sizes (for example 76 and 100 mm), ask for guidance: the right choice also depends on wall thickness and the material being wound.

All of these sizes are available from MilTube, with wall thickness adjustable from 3 to 17 mm and cut length up to 4 m. Custom sizes are available on request.
